3. Scatterplot Analysis

First, examine monthly income, age and education. Education is an ordinal variable, so its values appear in discrete bands.

pairs(
  ~ MonthlyIncome + Age + Education,
  data = hrdata,
  main = "Income, Age and Education"
)

Next, replace education with total working years to compare three numerical variables.

pairs(
  ~ MonthlyIncome + Age + TotalWorkingYears,
  data = hrdata,
  main = "Income, Age and Working Experience"
)

Independent-Samples t-Test

Test whether the mean age differs between employees with and without attrition.

yes_age <- hrdata[
  hrdata$Attrition == "Yes", "Age"
]

no_age <- hrdata[
  hrdata$Attrition == "No", "Age"
]

t.test(yes_age, no_age)
## 
##  Welch Two Sample t-test
## 
## data:  yes_age and no_age
## t = -5.828, df = 316.93, p-value = 1.38e-08
## alternative hypothesis: true difference in means is not equal to 0
## 95 percent confidence interval:
##  -5.288346 -2.618930
## sample estimates:
## mean of x mean of y 
##  33.60759  37.56123

The default Welch two-sample t-test evaluates whether the group means differ while allowing for unequal variances.

8. Interpretation and Limitations

The statistical results above should be interpreted by examining group means, effect sizes, p-values, confidence intervals and regression coefficients.

The analysis is observational. Associations do not establish causality, and omitted variables may influence the observed relationships.

The next stage is to interpret the actual statistical outputs and summarise the findings.
